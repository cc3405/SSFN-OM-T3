# Same-Day Procurement Run — $160 Hard Cap

**Status:** procurement checklist only; not a purchase order, verified bill of materials, or proof of rule compliance. No physical components have been built or tested.

## Hard constraints

- Total spend, including tax and any shipping, must not exceed **US$160**.
- Do not buy the previous $123.95 wheel-and-frame proposal. It is withdrawn because it leaves too little for brakes, axles, cart, coupling, guards, unloading device, and contingency.
- Do not use unverified casters, threaded rod, furniture hardware, or generic bicycle brake parts as a rider-rated vehicle system.
- Never compromise brakes, steering, axle retention, frame strength, seat/foot protection, or pinch guards to meet the budget.
- The cart-return inertia/lost-motion concept remains **unapproved and untested**. Do not buy a dedicated mechanism until the team has the official clarification and a bench prototype plan.

## Buy today only after measuring and inspecting the existing parts

1. **Inventory before leaving:** photograph and measure all existing wheels, hubs, axles, bearings, frame stock, steering, brake parts, shopping cart, fasteners, and plywood/wood. Record exact sizes, condition, maker/model, and any load rating.
2. **Wheels and axles:** prefer one matched, traceable, load-rated assembly with compatible hubs, axle diameter, bearing type, retention hardware, and mounting geometry. Obtain the manufacturer's load rating and confirm the rating applies to the actual mounting orientation and intended use. A product page price or a generic load figure alone is not sufficient.
3. **Brakes:** purchase only a complete compatible system designed for the chosen wheel/hub/frame geometry. Confirm mounting standard, rotor/rim compatibility, lever pull ratio, cable routing, and independent secure mounting. A bicycle disc-brake kit is not automatically suitable for a custom rideable vehicle.
4. **Cart and tow coupling:** use a sound existing/donated cart if possible. Confirm it rolls straight, has no sharp edges, and can be coupled without unstable side loads. Keep the coupling release accessible and guarded against accidental release.
5. **Unloader:** use low-cost wood/plywood already on hand where possible; build an independent manually operated stand only after measuring the cart and checkout area. Guard all pinch/shear points.
6. **Fasteners and guards:** buy only the measured quantities and grades needed. Use locking nuts/retainers where appropriate; protect exposed edges and moving joints.

## Same-day stop/go gates

- **GO:** only buy low-cost, non-structural consumables after the geometry is measured (bolts, washers, edge trim, guard material, paint/tape) and the total remains inside the budget.
- **HOLD:** wheels, axles, bearings, frame stock, steering, and brakes until compatibility and load suitability are checked together as a system.
- **STOP:** if the team cannot identify a safe, compatible, load-rated rider platform from existing/donated parts within the budget, do not improvise a rideable vehicle. Seek a donated complete cycle/ride-on chassis or re-plan with the coach before spending.

## Receipt and inspection record

For every purchase, record: store, date, item/model, quantity, pre-tax price, tax, total, manufacturer load rating, compatibility notes, and the person who inspected it. Keep receipts and packaging. Do not assemble or ride-test until the coach/team has reviewed the complete load path and braking system.

## Official rule dependency

The autonomous cart return remains a rule-interpretation risk. Get an official clarification on the inertia/lost-motion release concept before committing money to a custom release mechanism. A physical prototype test cannot replace an official ruling.
